THE EXPERIENCE · FOLLOW THE SIGNAL

Two signals. Two destinations. Players choose where to go, then discover the real science and history behind the machines they find.

01 / THE BACKGROUND

A hackathon challenge about forgotten space hardware.

I independently designed and developed MIRA Quest in 24 hours for the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge, responding to “Abandoned but not Forgotten,” a prompt to bring NASA equipment left behind on the Moon and Mars into an engaging educational experience.

Instead of presenting another timeline or information page, I wanted to explore how a player could discover the purpose, science, and legacy of a mission by interacting with its machine.

02 / THE PROBLEM

The science is fascinating. The way we encounter it can feel distant.

NASA's mission archives contain remarkable discoveries, photographs, and engineering stories, but for younger or casual audiences these can be scattered across technical articles, timelines, and databases. Knowing that a rover stopped transmitting is not the same as understanding what it achieved or why its story matters.

The design challenge was to make real space history approachable without oversimplifying the science—and make learning feel like an adventure, not homework.

03 / TARGET AUDIENCE

Designed for curious minds, not just space experts.

The primary audience is school-age space enthusiasts: learners who enjoy exploration, mysteries, games, and discovery. The experience also welcomes casual visitors of any age who may know the Moon landing or Mars rovers by name, but not the details behind them.

Short, approachable dialogue, visual investigation, clear choices, and collectible imagery offer different ways to engage with the material.

04 / THE SOLUTION

Let players uncover the mission, not just read about it.

MIRA Quest combines a cinematic first-person journey with conversational storytelling, environmental exploration, simple engineering puzzles, and authentic NASA imagery. Players follow signals to Mars and the Moon and meet two historic machines: Opportunity, the Mars rover, and Eagle, Apollo 11's lunar module.

Real NASA data is the foundation: Mission timelines, spacecraft specifications, scientific findings, and archival photographs come from NASA sources. The machines’ conversations are fictional storytelling devices, but the facts players uncover are grounded in actual missions. Player interaction connects the two.

05 / HOW IT WORKS

From a mysterious signal to a real discovery.

01 / THE JOURNEY

Follow the signal.

Players enter their names, answer a mysterious transmission, and choose a destination. Space travel, environmental scanning, and a search sequence build anticipation before the first encounter.

02 / MARS

Meet Opportunity.

Dialogue and investigations reveal how a planned 90-sol mission lasted 5,111 sols. Players examine evidence of ancient water, discover the rover's instruments, navigate the landscape, and explore authentic NASA photographs—through to the 2018 dust storm.

03 / THE MOON

Investigate Eagle.

Players explore the lunar module's ascent and descent stages and learn why part of Eagle remains on the Moon. A simplified landing exercise, mission photography, and lunar experiments make the engineering of Apollo 11 more tangible.

04 / THE ARCHIVE

Keep what you discover.

NASA photographs unlock in a collectible archive. Players can enlarge each image, read its context, and revisit what they learned. Completing both chapters reveals a tribute to the people behind these historic missions.

06 / DESIGN & IMPLEMENTATION

Built to work in the browser, not just in mockups.

Working solo within a 24-hour hackathon build, I translated the concept into a responsive game using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, integrating cinematic visuals, interactive panoramas, branching dialogue, image magnification, mini-games, ambient audio, and locally saved progress.

Keyboard navigation, sound controls, and reduced-motion settings were incorporated to make the experience more flexible. AI-assisted development helped move from story and interaction concepts to a functioning browser experience.

Educational boundaries: Character dialogue is dramatized, and engineering exercises are simplified for learning. Authentic NASA photographs and data are distinguished from AI-generated reconstructions.

07 / THE RESULT

Space history, experienced instead of skimmed.

In just 24 hours, I independently created a playable two-chapter educational adventure built on authentic NASA mission data and photographs. The result combines UX, interactive storytelling, visual design, and front-end development to make real space history engaging and memorable.
