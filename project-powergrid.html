PROJECT WALKTHROUGH

A walkthrough of the PowerGrid website design system.

01 / THE BRIEF

Sixteen websites, not sixteen separate design processes.

PowerGrid needed websites for 16 companies across its group. Each had its own logo, colors, typography, imagery, services, and content. My job was to create a system that could adapt to those differences without making every website a one-off build.

The client wanted something strong, modern, and straightforward—no unnecessary icons or decoration. I based the initial sections and placeholder content on our meetings and what was already available across the companies' sites, keeping everything flexible until we received final content.

Shared foundation

Reusable sections and layout patterns that make design and development more consistent.

Brand individuality

Logos, typography, color, imagery and content can change with each company.

Flexible content

Sections accommodate different service offerings and information without forcing every site into the same composition.

02 / THE SYSTEM

Three directions. Mix and match.

I developed three approaches, then made their sections interchangeable. The goal wasn't to pick A, B, or C and repeat it across 16 sites. We could combine sections from all three, alongside each company's branding and content, to make each website feel distinct.

A

Refined original

I polished the first direction and developed its pages, responsive layouts, and interactions.

B

Full alternative

A second design direction covering all pages, giving the team another complete set of sections.

C

Homepage exploration

A third homepage direction that adds even more section options to the system.

Designs A + B + C

Individual sections built to work together, not three rigid templates.

04 / DESIGN DETAILS

The system still had to work as real websites.

I kept the service layouts flexible for companies with different numbers of offerings, without hiding important content in a slider. I also made sure the individual company identity stayed prominent, while the PowerGrid connection remained clear.

Working in the browser helped me assess spacing, interactions, mobile layouts and implementation feasibility earlier, and iterate faster with the team.

05 / PRESENTATION FEEDBACK

What the client thought.

The three-direction concept and interactive demonstration received an enthusiastic response at the presentation. At that stage, final content and formal follow-up decisions were still pending; this was a working proposal for the wider 16-site project, not sixteen completed launches.
