THE STARTING POINT

A competitive RFP needed a thoughtful answer, not a prettier template.

Dalton came in through a win-back RFP, so I approached it as both a resident experience problem and a chance to show what a custom municipal site could be. I started with the town’s priorities, the information residents needed most, and the need to make a content-heavy public website feel clear rather than bureaucratic. Accessibility stayed in the design from the beginning: readable type, strong contrast, obvious interaction states, predictable navigation and layouts that would remain usable for residents with different ages and abilities.

THE PROCESS + RESULT

Three directions gave the client something real to react to.

I designed three distinct homepage directions instead of forcing an early answer, then presented the thinking behind each one. The client liked different strengths across the concepts, so I combined the best ideas into a cohesive final system and carried it through desktop, mobile and department pages. The committee was very pleased with the result and formally approved the homepage and department-page designs for development. The project reinforced how useful exploration can be when it is purposeful: more options early made the final direction clearer, not messier.
