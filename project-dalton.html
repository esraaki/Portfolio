01 / THE BRIEF

Not just a new look. A better way to reach the town.

My work started in the client discovery meetings, before opening Figma. Dalton already knew which services and quick links mattered most, had a preferred palette, and wanted a site that felt unmistakably theirs.

The brief also called for especially thoughtful accessibility, including keyboard-friendly navigation, clear information hierarchy, and interactions that wouldn't hide essential content behind hover effects. I studied the existing website and their reference sites, including Wellesley, to understand what to preserve and where to push further.

Resident-first access

Bring frequently needed information and services into view quickly, without forcing visitors to hunt through the site.

Accessible by design

Plan for clear contrast, readable content, keyboard interaction, and cues that don't depend on color alone.

Distinctly Dalton

Respect the town's preferences while moving beyond a one-size-fits-all municipal template.

02 / EXPLORATION

Three ways to interpret the same brief.

I proposed three complete visual directions, each exploring a different personality while keeping Dalton's content and usability requirements intact. I designed both a homepage and a department page for each. The committee liked elements across the concepts, so I combined their preferred parts into one cohesive final direction—not a patchwork of three designs.

The final direction: the town selected elements from multiple concepts. I refined their choices into a consistent visual system carried across the homepage, department pages, and responsive layouts. The approved result is shown at the top of this page.

03 / UX DECISIONS

Small decisions, big usability differences.

These are the details I designed around the way people actually use a municipal website.

01 / PRIORITIZATION

Two levels of public notices

Urgent safety alerts sit above routine town announcements, making the difference immediately visible.

02 / FINDABILITY

Less hero, more help

A deliberately shallow hero brings popular quick links above the fold, while persistent search keeps answers within reach.

03 / RECOGNITION

Services people can spot

Prominent icon links serve top resident tasks; a separate set of photographic cards makes other resources easy to recognize.

04 / CLARITY

Two calendars, two purposes

Community events and board or committee meetings use distinctly different layouts, with committee filtering for targeted browsing.

05 / ACCESSIBILITY

Not color alone

Calendar states include an explicit “Today” label, while clear link treatments, contrast, and keyboard-friendly behaviors support more users.

06 / IMPLEMENTATION

Beyond the Figma frame

I carried the system into responsive and department-page designs, collaborated with developers, and reviewed staging against the approved vision.

Approved by the people it was designed for.

The town's committee formally approved the homepage and department-page designs. The client also highlighted both the direction of the work and the care taken to incorporate their priorities. The project moved forward into implementation.
