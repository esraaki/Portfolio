01 / THE BRIEF

Starting with what Dalton needed.

I joined the project from the first client meetings. Dalton had already thought carefully about what should appear on the homepage, which links residents needed most, and the colors they wanted to use. I took those priorities into account before starting the designs.

Accessibility was a major part of those conversations. They wanted keyboard navigation to work well and didn't want important information to appear only on hover. I also reviewed their existing site and the Wellesley website they shared as a reference.

Easy access to services

Make the links people use most easy to find without excessive scrolling.

Accessibility from the start

Think about keyboard access, contrast, and clear labels during design—not just during testing.

A design that fits the town

Use the town's priorities and visual preferences instead of applying a ready-made look.

02 / EXPLORATION

Exploring three directions.

I suggested showing the client three different approaches rather than one initial design. Each included a homepage and a department page. They liked parts of all three, so I brought their preferred elements together and adjusted the details until the final design felt consistent.

What they chose: elements from all three directions. I combined and refined them into the final design shown above, then carried the same style through the department and mobile pages.

03 / UX DECISIONS

Why I designed it this way.

A few choices from the homepage that are easy to miss when you're only looking at the finished design.

Alerts have different priorities

I used a red bar for urgent warnings, such as severe weather, and a separate green bar for less urgent town updates. The red bar only appears when needed.

Useful links are visible sooner

The town wanted residents to see important services right away, so I kept the hero short and brought quick links above the fold. Search also stays in the header.

Different links, different treatments

I kept the town's requested icons for the main quick links, with small visual refinements. Other services use photos, making them easier to recognize at a glance.

The calendars serve different needs

Community events and board or committee meetings have separate visual treatments. I also added a committee filter to help visitors narrow down meetings.

Color isn't the only indicator

For example, the calendar marks the current date with “Today” as well as a color. I also considered contrast, visible links, and keyboard use throughout the layouts.

The work continued after approval

I carried the final direction into the department and responsive layouts, then collaborated with the developer and reviewed staging against the designs.

04 / OUTCOME

What the client said.

The committee approved both the homepage and department-page designs. Their feedback was especially meaningful because they had clear requirements from the start, and we had worked through several options together before settling on the final version.
