01 / THE CHALLENGE

Too much information. Not enough direction.

The old homepage had useful information, but it was difficult to scan. News, events, service links and a tightly packed calendar all demanded attention at once. I wanted people to find what they needed faster without stripping away the information the town had to provide.

THE REDESIGN / BEFORE & AFTER

Same town. A much clearer experience.

The old website is shown from a 2024 screenshot. The redesigned site on the right is the live custom work I designed.

What needed attention: information was present, but visitors had to scan competing sections and long text lists to find it.

What I changed: I reorganized the homepage around the tasks residents come for, while keeping deeper municipal content easy to reach.

02 / MY APPROACH

What I changed—and why.

Instead of redesigning around the town's internal structure, I looked at what residents are most likely to come to the website to do.

Common tasks up front

Agendas, minutes, forms, permits, and payments shouldn't require a long search through department pages. I gave these routes much more prominence.

Better content hierarchy

The old homepage gave many sections similar weight. I reorganized the layout so important information could stand out and supporting content was easier to scan.

Findability without guesswork

I kept navigation recognizable, with search and clearer routes into town information. The design needed to work for residents with very different levels of digital confidence.

Designed beyond desktop

I worked on responsive layouts, clear links, readable content and accessibility, and stayed involved with developers as the designs became a working website.
