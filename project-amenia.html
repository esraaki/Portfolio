01 / THE CHALLENGE

Too much information. Not enough direction.

The old homepage had plenty of useful information, but it asked visitors to sift through long news lists, event details, separate link groups, and a crowded calendar. When I started the redesign, I focused on making common tasks easier to spot without losing the government information people still needed.

BEFORE · THE OLD WEBSITE

A lot to read before you knew where to go.

  • Long, dense lists of town news and updates
  • Several competing sections with little separation
  • Small calendar and event details that were difficult to scan
  • Important actions mixed in with secondary content
AFTER · THE REDESIGN

Clearer paths to the things residents came for.

  • More obvious access to common services and information
  • Stronger hierarchy across homepage sections
  • Cleaner presentation of news, meetings, and events
  • Responsive layouts and familiar navigation patterns
02 / MY APPROACH

What I changed—and why.

Instead of redesigning around the town's internal structure, I looked at what residents are most likely to come to the website to do.

Common tasks up front

Agendas, minutes, forms, permits, and payments shouldn't require a long search through department pages. I gave these routes much more prominence.

Better content hierarchy

The old homepage gave many sections similar weight. I reorganized the layout so important information could stand out and supporting content was easier to scan.

Findability without guesswork

I kept navigation recognizable, with search and clearer routes into town information. The design needed to work for residents with very different levels of digital confidence.

Designed beyond desktop

I carried the custom direction through responsive layouts, accessibility considerations, and developer collaboration so the finished website worked beyond the original design file.
